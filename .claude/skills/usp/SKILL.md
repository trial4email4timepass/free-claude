# USP (Unique Selling Proposition)

## Goal
Identify one sharp, true, differentiated claim about why this product/brand is the better choice — not a laundry list of features.

## Inputs to gather (ask only if truly missing)
- Product/service and what it does
- Known alternatives/competitors
- Any data on what current customers say they love most (if available)

## Workflow
1. List candidate differentiators: what does this product do, have, or believe that alternatives genuinely don't?
2. Filter out differentiators that are (a) not actually true/defensible, or (b) true but not valued by the target audience — a real USP sits at the intersection of "true," "different," and "valued."
3. Pick the single strongest candidate and phrase it as a clear, specific claim — avoid vague superlatives ("the best," "world-class") in favor of a concrete mechanism or outcome.
4. Note 2-3 backup/secondary differentiators that support but don't dilute the primary USP.

## Output template
```markdown
# USP: <brand/product>

**Primary USP:** <one sharp, specific claim>

**Why it holds up:**
- True: <evidence>
- Different: <what alternatives lack>
- Valued: <why the audience cares>

**Secondary differentiators:**
- <point 1>
- <point 2>
```

## Avoid
- Vague claims ("quality," "innovative," "customer-focused") with no specificity
- A USP the audience doesn't actually care about, even if it's true and unique
- Listing five "unique" selling points instead of committing to one
