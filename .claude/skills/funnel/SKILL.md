# Funnel

## Goal
Map a realistic customer journey and funnel — concrete stages, what moves someone from one to the next, and where the likely drop-off points are.

## Inputs to gather (ask only if truly missing)
- Product/service and target audience
- Current acquisition channels, if any
- Whether this is a fast, low-consideration purchase or a longer, high-consideration one (changes funnel shape)

## Workflow
1. Define the funnel stages appropriate to the sales cycle (commonly: awareness → interest/consideration → conversion → retention/advocacy; adjust for the actual buying pattern).
2. For each stage, specify: what the customer is thinking/doing, the content/touchpoint that should meet them there, and the specific action that moves them forward.
3. Identify the most likely drop-off point and why (unclear value, friction, trust gap) and suggest what addresses it.
4. Don't stop at conversion — note what retention/advocacy looks like if relevant to the business model.

## Output template
```markdown
# Funnel: <product/service>

## Awareness
Customer state: <what they're thinking>
Touchpoint: <content/channel>
Moves them forward: <specific action/trigger>

## Consideration
...

## Conversion
...

## Retention/Advocacy
...

## Biggest drop-off risk
<stage> — <why> — <how to address it>
```

## Avoid
- Generic stage labels with no specifics on what happens at each
- Ignoring the drop-off analysis — that's the most actionable part
- Applying a long-consideration funnel to an impulse-buy product or vice versa
